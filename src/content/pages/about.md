This is Shafat M. In 2020, when I was home during the pandemic, I started a side hustle online, working as a management contributor in a well-known management forum. In May 2022, out of curiosity and a few small needs, I started doing affiliate marketing. I learned a lot from both. I continued my affiliate marketing side hustle till early 2025. But it was not satisfying for me at heart. So I kept searching something that could bring in $200–500 consistently every month, but also satisfy my heart. My goal was simple: I had roughly 2 hours a day and 5 days a week to work on a side hustle.

In early 2026, I decided I had to do something in open source. I have been using Linux-based systems for a decade. But I never really delved deep into it. All I did was distro hopping, a typical Linux fanboy habit that keeps a user changing distribution/desktop environment every once in a while. As I matured, I stopped distro hopping in the last few years because, while I was working as an affiliate marketer, I could recognize that this is just "shiny object syndrome" in a different form.

In the last 2 years, I realized that Linux has some potential to make money because it is used by most companies to run their servers. But it needs some know-how—doing the work others don't want to do. In my office, there are a few money plants in small cups. I found hardly anyone was responsible for watering those plants. So the plants go weak after a few weeks without water. Then someone waters them after seeing the weak plants. It happens again and again, almost every 2 weeks.

I started watering the plants out of curiosity—to see how often they actually need water. I thought about it like a manager: if it takes a lot of work, it needs a few people; if it takes very little work, one responsible person can handle it. I started doing it 2 years ago and couldn't stop. Turns out, it only needs water once a week.

I realized that to be successful in any side hustle, it should be like watering the plants—something no one wants to do, something I have to keep doing, and something that refreshes my heart. So, after talking with Claude for a while on 13 Aug 2026, I realized that Linux is something I should seriously learn from now on, and I'm aiming for Linux server maintenance and a retainer side hustle once I learn it. That's the beginning of the blog wateringtheplants. I'm hopeful that this time I'll be able to water the Linux plants regularly—and earn a living from it.
